---
category: Changed
subsection: Performance
---
- **A flush reads each message's payload once for its five Distribute stages (#1526).** PreDistribute (detached and
  inline), Distribute (detached) and PostDistribute (detached and inline) each parsed the stored payload again, five
  parses of the same bytes for every outbox and inbox message of every flush, whether or not a receptor existed for the
  stage. The stages of one flush now share one parse, and each still receives an envelope of its own, so hops and stamps
  one stage adds are not seen by another. Nothing is kept after the flush, and a payload that could not be read is read
  again by the next stage. One flush of 64 messages with a 1 KB payload allocates about a third of what it did and takes
  about half the time. **Upgrade:** the stages of a flush now receive the same payload object, so a Distribute-stage
  receptor that modifies the payload in place changes what the
  later stages of that flush see. Treat message payloads as immutable (records or init-only properties); a receptor
  that needs a changed copy makes one.
