- **Sequence integrity follows priority: an ambient scope, and cascades inherit their parent's setting (#2566).**
  `SequenceIntegrityContext.Enter(mode, holdTime)` sets the sequence integrity of every dispatch inside the scope
  that sets none itself, the way `PriorityContext.Enter` sets an ambient priority; disposing it restores the
  enclosing setting. What a receptor produces (an event it returns, that event's own cascades, and anything it
  dispatches while handling) inherits its parent dispatch's setting when the parent set one explicitly, through
  `DispatchOptions.WithSequenceIntegrity` or a scope. Precedence, most specific first: the dispatch's own options,
  the scope or inherited setting, the type's `[SequenceIntegrity]`, configuration, then `Bounded`; the hold time
  resolves the same way. Inheritance stays within the service: the setting is not carried on the wire. With nothing
  set explicitly, behavior is unchanged, and a command is still never held. Nothing to change.
