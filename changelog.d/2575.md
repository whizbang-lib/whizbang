- **A due schedule whose stream no live instance holds now fires, and one that stays unfired is reported (#2575).**
  The temporal engine claimed a due schedule only when its stream was already pinned to the claiming instance. A
  stream is pinned by its first write, and a schedule's first occurrence is that write, so a schedule created on a
  fresh stream (the framework's own integrity schedules among them) never fired on any instance, and neither did one
  whose stream's owner had died; nothing was logged. With ownership by map off, a schedule is now claimed by any instance
  when no live instance holds its stream; a stream held by another live instance stays that instance's, and each fire
  is still exactly-once. In map mode the claim already followed the partition's owner; the precise wake and the due
  poll now follow it too instead of the stream's pin, so such a schedule fires on time rather than on the backstop.
  Every drain pass also reads the Active schedules overdue by more than `Whizbang:Temporal:OverdueWarningMilliseconds`
  (default 60000, zero turns it off), publishes `whizbang.temporal.schedules.overdue` and
  `whizbang.temporal.schedules.overdue.oldest_age_seconds` on the `Whizbang.Temporal` meter, and logs a warning naming
  the oldest one when the overdue set appears or changes. **Upgrade:** a schedule that was stuck this way fires once on
  the first pass after the upgrade (its missed window coalesces under the default misfire policy). A custom
  `IScheduleClaimer` implements the new `GetOverdueSchedulesAsync`.
