category: Fixed
subsection: Work coordination, perspectives & sagas
---
- **A claim no longer waits on the planner-statistics refresh, and the maintenance index rebuild no longer waits on an
  old transaction for hours (#2611).** A concurrent rebuild of a queue index waits for every transaction older than it
  while holding a lock that blocks `ANALYZE`; behind an application transaction left idle, it held that lock for hours,
  the queue table's statistics stayed stale, and every claim queued on the refresh's lock and its five second lock
  timeout, so claims took seconds each. The refresh `claim_work` runs now takes each table's turn with
  `pg_try_advisory_xact_lock` (a claimant that finds it taken moves on), analyzes with `ANALYZE (SKIP_LOCKED)`, and after
  an analyze, a skip or a failure leaves the table alone for 60 seconds (`wh_queue_statistics_backoff`, migration 661),
  so a small queue table that churns hard, and reads as stale again right after every analyze, is analyzed at most once
  a minute instead of on nearly every claim; it still
  waits, for at most `deadlock_timeout` plus one second, when only an autovacuum holds the table, so PostgreSQL cancels
  a stuck worker as before. Each back-off is warned once, naming the table and the reason, and counted on
  `whizbang.work_coordinator.queue_statistics.refresh_skipped` (tags `table`, `reason`). The queue-index rebuild and the
  drop of a leftover now wait at most `Whizbang:Workers:Maintenance:ReindexLockTimeout` (default one minute) for each
  lock; a rebuild that gives up warns naming the index, each session it was waiting on and how long its transaction has
  been open, is counted on `whizbang.maintenance.queue_index_rebuild.gave_up` (tags `index`, `table`), drops the invalid
  copy it left at once, and is tried again on a later cycle. An invalid `_ccnew` or `_ccold` copy left by an earlier
  canceled rebuild is dropped by the next maintenance cycle. `IWorkCoordinator` gains `RebuildQueueIndexAsync` and a
  `DropInvalidQueueIndexAsync` overload that take the lock timeout; both have default implementations. Nothing to
  change.
