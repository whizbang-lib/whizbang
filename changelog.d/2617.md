---
category: Changed
subsection: Work coordination, perspectives & sagas
---
- **Every long-running loop waits on its wake sources through one primitive that leaves nothing behind (#2617).**
  A loop that raced fresh waits in `Task.WhenAny` left each losing wait registered where it waited until its token
  was canceled, which on a worker's stopping token is the life of the process (#728, #2613). The new public
  `WakeSet` (`Whizbang.Core.Async`, with `WakeSignal`) waits on channels, wake signals, one-shot tasks and semaphore
  permits together: one pending wait per source across passes, one timer on the injected `TimeProvider` stopped
  whenever another source wins, every wait released when it is disposed, and a permit acquired and not taken handed
  back exactly once. The perspective, dead-letter, duty-holder, heartbeat and startup loops, the batching windows,
  the perspective sync wait, the lease dispatch race and the work coordinator gate's interactive acquire all wait
  through it, with their behavior unchanged. Nothing to change.
- **The Azure Service Bus send timeout no longer leaves a timer running after each message (#2617).** The timeout was
  a separate delay raced against the send, which stayed armed for the full `SendTimeout` (30 s by default) after every
  publish. It is now the send's own deadline on the transport's `TimeProvider`, gone the moment the send completes; a
  send still pending at the deadline throws the same `TimeoutException`, now with the deadline's `TimeoutException` as
  its inner exception. **Breaking:** a publish whose `CancellationToken` is canceled while the send is in flight now
  throws `OperationCanceledException`. The raced delay ended on the same token and was reported as a send
  `TimeoutException`. **Upgrade:** code around `PublishAsync` (or the outbox's publish path) that treated a
  `TimeoutException` as "the caller canceled" catches `OperationCanceledException` for cancellation instead, and keeps
  `TimeoutException` for a send that outlived `SendTimeout`.
- **Repository check WHIZ611 is retired and replaced by WHIZ612 (#2617).** WHIZ611 reported a channel wait raced in
  `Task.WhenAny`; WHIZ612 reports any wait raced by hand (a delay, a channel wait, a semaphore wait, a completion
  source's task, a wake signal or a readiness gate) and accepts no hand-written shape. Both are repository-only
  analyzers that never ship in a package, so an application sees neither. **Upgrade:** a contributor removes any
  `WHIZ611` suppression or `.editorconfig`/`.globalconfig` entry (the ID is never reused) and moves a hand-written race
  in `src/` onto `WakeSet`; there is no suppression for WHIZ612. `PendingChannelWait<T>`, which the WHIZ611 fix added,
  is removed; it was internal, so nothing outside the library used it.
